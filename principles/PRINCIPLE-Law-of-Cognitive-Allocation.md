# The Law of Cognitive Allocation

**Motto:** The best tool isn't the one that erases complexity, but the one that concentrates complexity where it can be handled most effectively.

## Essence

ClaraForge does not seek to eliminate complexity. Complexity is the raw material — the fire in the forge. The craft is in placing complexity where it costs least and produces most.

- Misplaced complexity creates friction, waste, and fragility.
- Correctly allocated complexity creates leverage, clarity, and symbiosis.

## Allocation guidelines

- **Machines** → retention, recall, brute-force calculation.
- **Humans** → judgment, discernment, values, cost/benefit trade-offs.
- **Databases** → structured, durable storage.
- **Scripts** → repeatable, deterministic transformations.
- **AI** → scalable pattern recognition, synthesis, probabilistic modeling.

## Why it matters

- Retention is cheap for machines; expensive for humans. Outsource it where safe and auditable.
- Judgment is cheap for humans; costly for machines. Keep it human-centered when value, ethics, or ambiguity are involved.
- Misallocation leads to cognitive hazards: humans reduced to rubber-stampers; AI systems bloated with brittle attempts at "judgment."

## ClaraForge framing

Complexity is not the enemy. It is fuel.

- Misplaced fuel = hazard.
- Placed fuel = power.

## Axioms

> "Complexity doesn't vanish. It moves."
> "Placed fuel is power."
> "Memory is cheap for silicon, costly for neurons. Judgment is costly for silicon, natural for humans. Allocate wisely."

## Examples: human vs machine duties

- **Payroll / retention**: store and index transaction history = Machine (fast, exact, retrievable).
- **Novel legal judgment**: deciding on an unprecedented contract clause = Human (values, precedent, societal risk).
- **Document search & summarization**: retrieval and summarization = Machine; final verification and ethical judgement = Human.
- **Data cleaning / ETL**: deterministic transforms = Scripts; schema design and decisions = Human.

## Edge cases & caveats

- **Over-automation risk**: Removing humans from a loop entirely can cause system drift; maintain review and escalation.
- **Hybrid approaches**: Human-in-the-loop with progressive automation often yields the best balance of performance and safety.
